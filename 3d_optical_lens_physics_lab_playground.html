What's Happening Here?

The object is placed beyond 2F₁ of a converging (convex) lens. Refracted rays physically intersect on the opposite side between F₂ and 2F₂, creating a real, inverted, and smaller image—just like how a camera lens projects a scene onto a digital sensor!

Real-world use: Photographic Camera & Human Eye

Optical Lenses & Ray Tracing Cheat Sheet

Convex Lens (Converging)

Thicker at the center than at the edges. It bends parallel light rays inward so they converge at the real focal point F₂.

  • Focal length f is always Positive (+).
  • Forms Real & Inverted images when object is outside F₁.
  • Forms a Virtual, Upright & Magnified image when object is inside F₁.

Concave Lens (Diverging)

Thinner at the center than at the edges. It spreads parallel light rays outward so they appear to originate from the near focal point F₁.

  • Focal length f is always Negative (-).
  • Always forms a Virtual, Upright & Diminished image on the same side as the object, regardless of object distance.

The 3 Principal Rays for Constructing Any Image

Ray 1 (Red): Starts parallel to the principal axis. After refraction, passes through F₂ (Convex) or appears to diverge from F₁ (Concave).
Ray 2 (Green): Passes straight through the Optical Center (O) of the lens without bending.
Ray 3 (Amber): Passes through F₁ (Convex) or aims toward F₂ (Concave) before hitting the lens, then refracts parallel to the principal axis.

Cartesian Sign Convention

All distances are measured from the Optical Center O (0,0) with incident light traveling left-to-right:

Left of O: Negative (-)
Right of O: Positive (+)
Above Axis: Positive (+)
Below Axis: Negative (-)
